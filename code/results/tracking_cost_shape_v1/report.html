Does historical cost shape help beyond its level?

Three paired BO arms share the source-derived catalogue, six initial target tests, frozen source histories, target learning, GP settings and rollout budgets. Only the historical log-cost prior mean changes. Historical feasibility rates are neutral (.5); target feasibility learning remains active in every BO arm.

No additional prior: zero log-cost mean. Context level: the uniform average of the frozen historical GP mean across all 71 gains in each context, repeated for every gain in that context. Full prior: the unchanged gain-dependent historical mean. The context-level arm preserves each context's average log-cost exactly. This is a geometric cost level, not an arithmetic mean of J. No target data or validation outcome enters that average.

Interpretation and stopping point

A benefit of full over level supports useful gain-dependent historical information under this optimizer. If level reproduces the benefit, the evidence supports centering rather than a need for detailed historical cost shape. Mixed outcomes should be described by vehicle, quality and failed-query tradeoffs.

The catalogue still uses historical costs and eligibility, and source cost GPs were fitted to eligible observations. This is a conditional attribution after common initialization. History is data-rich, the 71-point gain pool and references are fixed, and eligibility is not a stability certificate. No new physical rollouts, sparse-history study or networked federated protocol is introduced.

After these results, consolidate the complete evidence: what was learned, which claims survive, what remains unsupported, and whether the task justifies further federated controller-tuning work. Do not automatically expand the experiment chain. See docs/tracking_cost_shape.md.
