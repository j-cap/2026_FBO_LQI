\begin{abstract}
Controller calibration from closed-loop experiments is naturally distributed across fleets of related but dynamically heterogeneous systems. Bayesian optimization (BO) can tune low-dimensional controller-design parameters with few experiments, while collaborative BO can reuse information across clients. In heterogeneous fleets, however, indiscriminate sharing can cause negative transfer. This paper studies dynamics-aware federated Bayesian optimization for LQI controller calibration. Each client identifies a compact local model and covariance, and pairwise uncertainty-aware dynamics distance is converted into a continuous similarity weight for cross-client BO observations. A clean vehicle benchmark is developed in which every client is baseline-operable and is evaluated on the same balanced multi-maneuver task distribution. On twenty untouched fleet seeds, always-on similarity-weighted sharing shows a modest favorable tendency but does not clear the pre-registered superiority criterion over independent BO. A stronger mechanism emerges: an early local-search stagnation signal computed only from data available up to evaluation 11--15 prospectively predicts benefit realized strictly afterward. The seed-level effect is positive at every tested cutoff and strongest at evaluation 15. These results motivate a need-aware extension in which dynamics similarity determines \emph{whom} to trust, while local search stagnation determines \emph{when} collaboration should be activated. \draftnote{The adaptive extension is the next locked experiment and must be validated on a fresh untouched seed set before the final method claim is made.}
\end{abstract}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
Modern fleets of vehicles, robots, energy systems, and industrial assets contain many systems that perform the same control task but differ in their local dynamics. These differences arise from payload, component variation, degradation, operating conditions, and unmodeled plant effects. A controller calibration that is effective for one client can therefore be suboptimal for another. At the same time, controller calibration is expensive because every candidate must ultimately be judged from a closed-loop experiment or high-fidelity rollout.

Federated learning provides a general framework for exploiting distributed data while keeping raw datasets local~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}. For control, however, heterogeneity is not merely a non-IID sampling issue: clients can represent genuinely different dynamical systems. Information transfer is therefore useful only when the donor and recipient are sufficiently related in a control-relevant sense.

Our previous work addressed a related problem through uncertainty-aware clustered federated system identification~\cite{weber2026ifac}. Each client performs local identification and communicates an estimated model together with its covariance. This reveals reusable dynamical structure across the fleet and supports model-based controller synthesis. It does not remove the subsequent LQR/LQI calibration problem, because the state and input weighting matrices still need to be selected.

Bayesian optimization (BO) is attractive for this calibration problem because closed-loop evaluations are expensive, noisy, and generally available only as zeroth-order measurements of task performance~\cite{frazier2018tutorial}. BO-based controller tuning has been demonstrated for LQR design~\cite{marco2016lqr}, safe controller optimization~\cite{berkenkamp2016safe,berkenkamp2023safe}, automotive PI tuning~\cite{schillinger2017safe}, and LQI/MPC tuning with constraints~\cite{vonrohr2024local}. Standard BO nevertheless treats each controlled system independently.

Federated and collaborative BO seek to reuse black-box information across agents~\cite{dai2020fts,dai2021dpfts,chen2025collaborative}. In heterogeneous control fleets, the unresolved question is not simply whether information can be shared, but \emph{which clients should trust each other and when collaboration is actually needed}. We exploit physical side information that generic FBO does not use explicitly: each client already possesses an identified dynamics model and an estimate of its uncertainty.

This motivates the first mechanism studied here,
\begin{equation}
    \distdyn_{ij}\uparrow
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \text{controller-calibration transfer becomes less reliable},
    \label{eq:intro_hypothesis}
\end{equation}
where $\distdyn_{ij}$ is an uncertainty-aware distance between the identified dynamics of clients $i$ and $j$. Rather than converting this information into hard controller clusters, we use it as a continuous relevance signal.

The clean confirmatory experiments reveal a second, equally important point: relevance alone is insufficient. Always-on similarity-weighted sharing is directionally favorable but does not reliably beat independent BO at the pre-registered seed-level criterion. In contrast, local-search stagnation measured early in the independent BO trajectory prospectively predicts which clients will benefit later from sharing. This leads to the central distinction of the present paper:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\text{dynamics similarity } s_{ij}\Rightarrow\text{whom to trust}}
\end{equation}
versus
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\text{local stagnation } D_i\Rightarrow\text{when to collaborate}.}
\end{equation}

The contributions of the current manuscript are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate heterogeneous fleet controller calibration as a collaborative BO problem in which the objective is reduced local experiment count for reaching each client's own high-quality LQI calibration.
    \item We introduce an uncertainty-aware dynamics similarity derived from local model estimates and covariance and use it as a continuous weight on cross-client BO observations.
    \item We develop a clean benchmark protocol that removes two confounds discovered during the study: baseline-infeasible clients that distorted pooled GP scales and slot-dependent lane-change tasks that changed the BO landscape for reasons unrelated to dynamics.
    \item We show on twenty untouched fleet seeds that always-on similarity weighting does not meet the pre-registered superiority criterion over independent BO, despite a modest favorable tendency.
    \item We show with seed-aware statistics and a strictly prospective test that early local-search stagnation predicts future benefit from similarity sharing, including after controlling for vehicle family.
    \item We formulate the resulting need-aware extension, in which dynamics similarity determines peer relevance and a stagnation trigger determines whether peer information is activated. \draftnote{The final adaptive-method claim is pending untouched-seed validation.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
Consider $N$ clients indexed by $i\in\mathcal C=\{1,\dots,N\}$. The true plant of client $i$ is
\begin{align}
    x_{i,k+1} &= f_i(x_{i,k},u_{i,k},w_{i,k}),\\
    y_{i,k} &= h_i(x_{i,k},v_{i,k}),
\end{align}
where $f_i$ differs across clients. Each client fits a compact local model
\begin{equation}
    x_{k+1}=A(\theta_i)x_k+B(\theta_i)u_k
\end{equation}
and exposes an estimate $\hat\theta_i$ together with covariance $\hat\Sigma_i$. Raw identification trajectories remain local.

For the identified model $(\hat A_i,\hat B_i,\hat C_i)$, augment the state with integral tracking error and parameterize the LQI cost by $\xi\in\Xi\subset\mathbb R^d$,
\begin{equation}
    Q(\xi)=\mathrm{diag}(10^{\xi_1},\ldots,10^{\xi_d}),\qquad R=I.
\end{equation}
Solving the DARE yields the client-specific controller
\begin{equation}
    K_i(\xi)=\mathrm{LQI}(\hat A_i,\hat B_i,\hat C_i,Q(\xi),R).
\end{equation}
Thus federation transfers information in the common design space $\xi$; a fixed feedback gain is never transferred.

For calibration episode $\omega$, applying $K_i(\xi)$ to the true plant gives tracking loss $J_i(\xi;\omega)$. In the clean benchmark every client is evaluated on the same balanced two-maneuver task distribution,
\begin{equation}
    f_i(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb E J_i(\xi\mid T_1)+\mathbb E J_i(\xi\mid T_2)\right],
    \label{eq:balanced_objective}
\end{equation}
implemented with three calibration episodes per maneuver, i.e. six episodes per BO query. The same balanced structure is used for held-out evaluation.

A sampled client is admitted only when the fixed baseline calibration $\xi^{\mathrm{base}}$ is feasible on the standardized multi-maneuver episode bank. This guarantees that all accepted clients are initially operable and that their normalized BO observations live on a comparable scale.

The objective of federation is not to force a common optimum. Instead, collaboration should reduce the number of local plant evaluations needed for each client to approach its own best feasible calibration
\begin{equation}
    \xi_i^\star\in\arg\min_{\xi\in\feasSet_i}f_i(\xi).
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Dynamics-Aware Federated Bayesian Optimization}
\label{sec:method}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Uncertainty-aware dynamics similarity}
For clients $i$ and $j$, define
\begin{equation}
    (\distdyn_{ij})^2=(\hat\theta_i-\hat\theta_j)^\top
    (\hat\Sigma_i+\hat\Sigma_j+\epsilon I)^{-1}
    (\hat\theta_i-\hat\theta_j),
    \label{eq:dyn_distance}
\end{equation}
and map this distance to
\begin{equation}
    s_{ij}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\distdyn_{ij})^2}{2\ell_d^2}\right),
    \label{eq:similarity}
\end{equation}
with $\ell_d$ selected by the median pairwise-distance heuristic.

\subsection{Static similarity-weighted FBO}
Each target client fits a GP surrogate over the common LQI search space and uses expected improvement to select the next candidate. Cross-client observation relevance is implemented through heteroscedastic observation noise. The static rule is
\begin{equation}
    w^{\mathrm{static}}_{i\leftarrow j}=
    \begin{cases}
        1, & j=i,\\
        s_{ij}, & j\neq i.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:static_weight}
\end{equation}
The independent baseline uses only $j=i$, whereas global pooling assigns weight one to all observations.

\subsection{Need-aware adaptive extension}
The static confirmatory experiment motivates an additional local collaboration-need variable. Let $D_i(c)$ denote the fraction of post-initialization transitions up to cutoff $c$ in which the incumbent of client $i$ does not improve. The prospective analysis considers $c\in\{11,13,15\}$ and finds the cleanest seed-level relation at $c=15$.

The proposed adaptive rule is
\begin{equation}
    w^{\mathrm{adapt}}_{i\leftarrow j}(n)=
    \begin{cases}
        1, & j=i,\\
        a_i(n)s_{ij}, & j\neq i,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:adaptive_weight}
\end{equation}
with one-time trigger
\begin{equation}
    a_i(n)=
    \begin{cases}
        0, & n\le c,\\
        \mathbb I[D_i(c)\ge\tau], & n>c.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:trigger_rule}
\end{equation}
The current development plan freezes $c=15$ and chooses $\tau$ from a small prespecified candidate set on development seeds only. Once sharing is activated, all peer observations available at that time may enter the target surrogate with dynamics weights $s_{ij}$. \draftnote{Do not convert this into a result statement until the fresh adaptive confirmatory campaign is complete.}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
The final static-sharing benchmark contains 30 clients, ten from each of three generating families: nominal, payload, and tire-degraded. All clients operate at the same nominal speed. The true plant is a nonlinear lateral vehicle model with tire saturation, while identification and LQI synthesis retain a linear two-state lateral model.

The versioned v3 generator uses a common task distribution for every client. All clients see both lane-change maneuvers, each BO query averages three episodes per maneuver, and the baseline-feasibility admission test is evaluated on the same multi-maneuver bank. The resulting accepted fleet retains the intended dynamics heterogeneity while avoiding task-induced clustering artifacts and baseline-normalization fallbacks.

The clean static confirmatory campaign uses twenty entirely new fleet seeds, eight initial evaluations, and a total budget of 25 local evaluations per client. Performance is summarized using oracle-relative regret
\begin{equation}
    r_i^\star(n)=\frac{J_i^{\mathrm{best}}(n)-J_i^\star}{J_i^\star},
    \label{eq:regret_star}
\end{equation}
and its area under the regret curve, $\AURCstar$. Fleet seed, not individual client, is the method-level unit of replication.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Static similarity weighting is not a reliable universal improvement}
On the clean v3 campaign, the paired seed-level $\AURCstar$ difference between similarity FBO and independent BO is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{median}_s\Delta_s^{\mathrm{sim-ind}}=-0.0098,
\end{equation}
with bootstrap 95\% CI $(-0.0347,0.0000)$. Twelve of twenty seeds are favorable and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives $p=0.121$. The tail condition is not problematic: similarity's pooled-client $p90$ $\AURCstar$ is 0.395 versus 0.450 for independent BO. The correct interpretation is therefore a modest favorable tendency, not a reproducible fleet-agnostic win.

This result is important because both major benchmark confounds identified during development have been removed. Baseline-infeasible clients no longer introduce incompatible GP output scales, and all clients are evaluated on the same balanced multi-maneuver task distribution. The near-null result should therefore be treated as substantive evidence that relevance-aware sharing alone is insufficient.

\subsection{Search stagnation predicts future benefit from sharing}
The mechanism changes when collaboration need is considered explicitly. In a seed-aware analysis, the within-seed Spearman relation between stagnation $D_i$ and realized sharing benefit has median
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{median}(\rho_s)=0.228,
\end{equation}
with 95\% CI $(0.122,0.488)$, 16/20 seeds positive, and Wilcoxon $p=0.0032$. Residualizing within seed-by-family cells yields an even stronger association, and every family separately shows a positive relation.

More importantly, the relation survives a genuinely prospective test with zero window overlap. Let $D_i(c)$ use only data up to cutoff $c$ and define strictly future sharing benefit
\begin{equation}
    G_i^{\mathrm{future}}(c)=\sum_{n=c+1}^{25}
    \left[r_{i,\mathrm{ind}}^\star(n)-r_{i,\mathrm{sim}}^\star(n)\right].
    \label{eq:future_gain}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Implication: combine collaboration need and peer relevance}
The results suggest a simple decomposition. Dynamics similarity remains useful for deciding \emph{which} peer observations are relevant. But static use of that information does not improve every client because many clients already converge efficiently with independent BO. Local stagnation provides the missing \emph{need} signal. Equation~\eqref{eq:adaptive_weight} therefore activates dynamics-aware sharing only for clients whose local search is struggling.

The present twenty-seed v3 campaign is now treated as development data for the adaptive extension. The cutoff is frozen at $c=15$. A small prespecified threshold set is used to choose $\tau$, after which the complete adaptive rule, primary metric, and seed-level statistical analysis are frozen before a fresh untouched twenty-seed confirmatory campaign.

\subsection{Limitations and next validation step}
The current positive mechanism result does not yet establish that the adaptive algorithm improves fleet-level performance. The current v3 seeds have been inspected and therefore serve only as development data for choosing the trigger threshold. The final method claim requires a fresh untouched seed set. The study also remains simulation based, uses a low-dimensional LQI calibration space, and shares scalar BO observations even though raw trajectories remain local. No differential-privacy claim is made.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
This paper studies collaborative LQI calibration in heterogeneous dynamical fleets. Uncertain identified dynamics provide a physically meaningful measure of cross-client relevance, but the clean confirmatory experiment shows that always-on similarity-weighted sharing is not a reliable universal improvement over independent BO. A stronger result emerges from the optimization trajectory itself: local-search stagnation measured early in the run prospectively predicts the benefit of sharing that occurs strictly later, under seed-aware and family-controlled analyses. This supports a need-aware formulation in which dynamics similarity determines \emph{whom} to trust and stagnation determines \emph{when} collaboration is activated. \draftnote{Replace the final sentence with the untouched-seed adaptive-method result once that campaign is complete.}
