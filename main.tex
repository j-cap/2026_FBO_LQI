\begin{abstract}
Controller calibration from closed-loop experiments is naturally distributed in fleets of related but dynamically heterogeneous systems. Bayesian optimization (BO) can tune controller parameters with few experiments, while collaborative or federated BO can reduce local evaluation effort by reusing information across clients. The central difficulty is negative transfer: observations collected on one plant need not be equally informative for another. This paper develops dynamics-aware federated Bayesian optimization (\method{}) for model-based controller calibration. Each client identifies a compact local model and covariance; the server converts pairwise uncertainty-aware model distances into continuous similarity weights that determine how strongly peer BO observations influence a target client's Gaussian-process surrogate. The controller itself is never transferred: every candidate LQI gain is synthesized from the target client's identified model and evaluated on its local plant. The approach is studied on heterogeneous vehicle lateral dynamics with a nonlinear true plant and a linear identified control-design model. Mechanism experiments show that identified dynamics distance is positively associated with controller-transfer loss, while hard cluster-specific calibration provides little consistent average benefit. In a preliminary corrected medium-scale study with 30 clients and five independent seeds, similarity-weighted FBO reduces median normalized simple regret from $0.026$ to $0.007$ at 15 local evaluations and from $0.0053$ to $0.0014$ at 20 evaluations relative to independent BO; indiscriminate global pooling is consistently worse in the later search stage. \draftnote{Replace the quantitative medium-scale statement by the final 15--20-seed campaign before submission.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
Modern fleets of vehicles, robots, energy systems, and industrial assets contain many systems that perform the same control task but differ in their local dynamics. These differences arise from payload, component variation, degradation, operating conditions, and unmodeled plant effects. A controller calibration that is effective for one client can therefore be suboptimal for another. At the same time, controller calibration is expensive because every candidate must ultimately be judged from a closed-loop experiment or high-fidelity rollout. The resulting data are naturally distributed across clients and may be costly or undesirable to centralize at trajectory level.

Federated learning provides a general framework for exploiting distributed data while keeping raw datasets at the clients~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}. For control, however, heterogeneity is not merely a non-IID sampling issue: clients can represent genuinely different dynamical systems. Information transfer is therefore useful only if the donor and recipient are sufficiently related in a control-relevant sense. This distinction is especially important for controller calibration, where a harmful transfer does not merely degrade a prediction model but can direct an expensive closed-loop experiment toward an unhelpful or infeasible controller.

Our previous work addressed a related problem through uncertainty-aware clustered federated system identification~\cite{weber2026ifac}. Each client performs local recursive identification and communicates an estimated model together with its covariance. Uncertainty-aware distances in parameter space reveal reusable dynamical structure across the fleet and can support model-based controller synthesis. This pipeline answers which dynamics are similar, but it does not determine the LQR/LQI weighting matrices that yield the best task-level closed-loop performance on the actual plant. Even when the controller structure remains model based, those weights are commonly selected manually or through repeated simulation.

Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to this calibration problem because closed-loop controller evaluations are expensive, noisy, and generally available only as zeroth-order measurements of task performance~\cite{frazier2018tutorial}. BO-based automatic controller tuning has been demonstrated for LQR design~\cite{marco2016lqr}, safe controller optimization~\cite{berkenkamp2016safe,berkenkamp2023safe}, automotive PI calibration~\cite{schillinger2017safe}, and LQI/MPC tuning with crash constraints~\cite{vonrohr2024local}. These works show that model-based controller structure can be retained while low-dimensional design parameters are refined from experiments. Standard BO, however, treats each controlled system independently.

Federated and collaborative BO seek to reuse black-box information across multiple agents. Federated Thompson sampling (FTS) demonstrated that distributed agents can benefit from surrogate information produced by peers, including under heterogeneous objectives~\cite{dai2020fts}; differentially private extensions further formalize user-level privacy~\cite{dai2021dpfts}. Related collaborative BO approaches explicitly model heterogeneous agents and selective information transfer~\cite{chen2025collaborative}. The unresolved question for heterogeneous control fleets is therefore not whether BO information can be shared, but \emph{how strongly a target client should trust an observation collected on another dynamical system}.

We exploit a source of structure that generic FBO does not use explicitly: every client already possesses an identified dynamics model and an estimate of its uncertainty. This motivates the hypothesis
\begin{equation}
    \distdyn_{ij} \uparrow
    \quad \Longrightarrow \quad
    \text{controller-calibration transfer becomes less reliable},
    \label{eq:intro_hypothesis}
\end{equation}
where $\distdyn_{ij}$ is an uncertainty-aware distance between the identified dynamics of clients $i$ and $j$. Rather than converting this information into hard controller clusters, we use it as a continuous relevance signal. Nearby clients contribute strongly to each other's BO surrogates, whereas observations from dynamically distant clients are down-weighted.

This choice is supported by our mechanism experiments. After correcting the LQI normalization, held-out evaluation protocol, and physical constraints, dynamics distance consistently predicts controller-transfer degradation. On the final fixed-speed fleet, the relation remains moderate but statistically clear, while transfer itself is asymmetric: for example, payload clients can be vulnerable recipients of calibrations obtained on lighter vehicles. A recipient-side directional gate was therefore investigated as an additional mechanism. The subsequent BO experiment, however, shows that the simpler symmetric similarity weighting already provides the useful sample-efficiency gain, whereas the extra gate does not improve optimization. We therefore retain the dynamics similarity as the principal method and treat recipient-aware gating as an ablation.

The resulting framework, termed \emph{dynamics-aware federated Bayesian optimization} (\method{}), keeps raw identification and closed-loop trajectories local. Clients share uncertain model descriptors and low-dimensional BO observations in the common controller-weight space. For every target client, peer observations are incorporated into a Gaussian-process surrogate with a weight determined by identified dynamics similarity. Expected improvement (EI) then proposes a new LQI weighting, from which the target client synthesizes its own gain and performs its own constrained plant evaluation. Figure~\ref{fig:method_overview} summarizes this information flow.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate heterogeneous fleet controller calibration as a collaborative black-box optimization problem in which the objective is not one fleet-wide optimum, but reduced local experiment count for reaching each client's own high-quality LQI calibration.
    \item We introduce an uncertainty-aware dynamics similarity derived from local model estimates and covariance, and use it directly as a continuous weight on cross-client BO observations. This avoids both indiscriminate global pooling and hard cluster assignment.
    \item We develop a common weighted-GP/EI calibration framework in which independent BO, global pooling, similarity-weighted FBO, and a recipient-aware extension differ only in their cross-client observation weights, enabling a controlled ablation of the information-transfer mechanism.
    \item We provide mechanism evidence that identified dynamics distance predicts controller-transfer degradation on matched and mismatched plants, and preliminary BO evidence that similarity-weighted FBO improves finite-budget convergence while global pooling produces negative transfer. \draftnote{Replace preliminary medium-scale numbers by the final statistically replicated campaign before submission.}
\end{itemize}

The work is related to federated LQR for heterogeneous dynamical systems~\cite{wang2026fedlqr}, but differs in objective and architecture. Federated LQR directly learns control policies from distributed interactions. Here, local system identification remains the basis for model-based LQI synthesis; federation acts only on the low-dimensional black-box calibration problem that remains after the model has been identified.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Heterogeneous clients and local identification}
Consider $N$ clients indexed by $i\in\mathcal C=\{1,\dots,N\}$. The true plant of client $i$ is
\begin{align}
    x_{i,k+1} &= f_i(x_{i,k},u_{i,k},w_{i,k}),\\
    y_{i,k} &= h_i(x_{i,k},v_{i,k}),
\end{align}
where the maps $f_i$ may differ across clients. Each client fits a compact local model
\begin{equation}
    x_{k+1}=A(\theta_i)x_k+B(\theta_i)u_k
\end{equation}
from local identification data and exposes an estimate $\hat\theta_i$ with covariance $\hat\Sigma_i$. The true plant and the control-design model need not have identical structure. In the main benchmark, the true plant uses smooth nonlinear tire saturation, while identification and controller synthesis retain a linear two-state lateral model.

\subsection{LQI calibration space}
For the identified model $(\hat A_i,\hat B_i,\hat C_i)$, augment the state with integral tracking error
\begin{equation}
    z_{k+1}=z_k+T_s(r_k-\hat C_i x_k).
\end{equation}
With augmented state $x_k^a=[x_k^\top,z_k^\top]^\top$, use explicit physical normalization
\begin{equation}
    \tilde x_k^a=S_x^{-1}x_k^a,
    \qquad S_x=\mathrm{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_{n_a}).
\end{equation}
The tunable LQI cost is parameterized by $\xi\in\Xi\subset\mathbb R^d$,
\begin{align}
    Q(\xi)&=\mathrm{diag}(10^{\xi_1},\ldots,10^{\xi_d}),\\
    R&=I.
\end{align}
Fixing one global cost scale removes the standard LQR/LQI scaling redundancy. Solving the DARE yields a client-specific controller
\begin{equation}
    K_i(\xi)=\mathrm{LQI}(\hat A_i,\hat B_i,\hat C_i,Q(\xi),R).
\end{equation}
Thus federation transfers information in the common design space $\xi$; it never transfers a fixed feedback gain.

For calibration episode $\omega$, applying $K_i(\xi)$ to the true plant gives tracking loss $J_i(\xi;\omega)$ and black-box objective
\begin{equation}
    f_i(\xi)=\mathbb E_\omega[J_i(\xi;\omega)].
\end{equation}
In the vehicle benchmark, $J_i$ is yaw-rate tracking RMSE. Steering magnitude, steering rate, sideslip, and controller-synthesis/closed-loop failures define feasibility.

\subsection{Finite-budget collaborative objective}
Let
\begin{equation}
    \xi_i^\star\in\arg\min_{\xi\in\feasSet_i} f_i(\xi)
\end{equation}
denote the best feasible calibration for client $i$. The purpose of federation is not to force a common $\xi^\star$, but to reduce the number of local plant evaluations needed to approach $f_i(\xi_i^\star)$. The primary quantity of interest is therefore finite-budget simple regret rather than only asymptotic final performance.

\section{Dynamics-Aware Federated Bayesian Optimization}
\label{sec:method}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Uncertainty-aware dynamics similarity}
For clients $i$ and $j$, define the pairwise dynamics distance
\begin{equation}
    (\distdyn_{ij})^2=(\hat\theta_i-\hat\theta_j)^\top
    (\hat\Sigma_i+\hat\Sigma_j+\epsilon I)^{-1}
    (\hat\theta_i-\hat\theta_j).
    \label{eq:dyn_distance}
\end{equation}
The covariance term makes a parameter difference less decisive when either client's estimate is uncertain. The distance is mapped to a continuous similarity
\begin{equation}
    s_{ij}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\distdyn_{ij})^2}{2\ell_d^2}\right),
    \label{eq:similarity}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_d$ is selected by the median pairwise-distance heuristic. We use $s_{ii}=1$.

The central design decision is to retain this continuous score rather than thresholding it into clusters. A target client can therefore exploit useful nearby peers without treating all members of a cluster as identical.

\subsection{Normalized shared observations}
Client $j$ produces BO observations
\begin{equation}
    \calD_j=\{(\xi_{j,m},\tilde y_{j,m})\}_{m=1}^{M_j},
\end{equation}
where the objective is normalized by a client-local baseline controller,
\begin{equation}
    \tilde y_{j,m}=\frac{J_j(\xi_{j,m})}{J_j^{\mathrm{base}}}.
    \label{eq:normalized_objective}
\end{equation}
The baseline is available before BO and does not use oracle information. Infeasible evaluations are represented by a fixed normalized penalty so that infeasibility remains visible to the surrogate without introducing oracle leakage. Oracle values are reserved for post-hoc regret and target-reaching metrics.

For target client $i$, all available scalar BO observations form a pooled set, but each observation from client $j$ receives target-specific weight
\begin{equation}
    w_{i\leftarrow j}=s_{ij}.
    \label{eq:similarity_weight}
\end{equation}
The independent baseline uses $w_{i\leftarrow j}=\mathbb I[i=j]$, whereas global pooling uses $w_{i\leftarrow j}=1$ for all clients.

\subsection{Weighted Gaussian-process surrogate}
Each target client fits a Gaussian-process surrogate over the common LQI search space. We implement observation relevance through heteroscedastic observation noise: a low-weight peer point is treated as less trustworthy than a local or dynamically similar point. For base numerical noise $\alpha_0$,
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{i,j,m}=\frac{\alpha_0}{\max(w_{i\leftarrow j},\epsilon_w)}.
    \label{eq:weighted_alpha}
\end{equation}
Hence local observations have the strongest influence, close peers contribute almost normally, and distant peers approach high-noise pseudo-observations. The current implementation uses a Gaussian-process regressor with a Mat\'ern kernel and expected improvement (EI) to select the next candidate
\begin{equation}
    \xi_{i,n+1}\in\arg\max_{\xi\in\Xi}\mathrm{EI}_i(\xi\mid\cup_j\calD_j,w_{i\leftarrow j}).
\end{equation}
Rounds are synchronous: every proposal in round $n$ uses only observations available at the end of round $n-1$. This keeps the information pattern identical across compared methods.

This implementation differs from FTS~\cite{dai2020fts}: we use the same federated/collaborative BO motivation, but transfer low-dimensional scalar BO observations and encode relevance directly in the target surrogate instead of communicating random-feature Thompson samples. Raw system-identification and closed-loop trajectories remain local. Accordingly, the method provides data locality at trajectory level, not formal privacy of scalar objective values.

\subsection{Recipient-aware extension as an ablation}
Phase-1 diagnostics showed that controller-transfer loss can be directional even though $\distdyn_{ij}$ is symmetric. We therefore tested an additional recipient-conditioned gate. For candidate $\xi$, the recipient's own identified model predicts the maximum sideslip $\max|\hat\beta_i(\xi)|$, leading to
\begin{equation}
    g_i(\xi)=
    \begin{cases}
    0, & \text{if the recipient-model closed loop is nominally unstable},\\[1mm]
    \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\max|\hat\beta_i(\xi)|}{\beta_{\max}}\right)^2\right], & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The corresponding weight is
\begin{equation}
    w_{i\leftarrow j}(\xi)=s_{ij}g_i(\xi).
\end{equation}
This extension is included as an ablation rather than the main method because the corrected medium-scale BO results do not show an improvement over the simpler similarity weighting.

\subsection{Communication and scope}
The current study does not claim differential privacy or secure aggregation. Clients retain raw identification and closed-loop trajectory data locally, but share $(\hat\theta_i,\hat\Sigma_i)$ and scalar controller-calibration observations $(\xi,J_i(\xi))$ required by the collaborative surrogate. Differentially private FBO mechanisms such as~\cite{dai2021dpfts} could be added in future work.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Vehicle fleet and model mismatch}
The main evaluation uses a fixed-speed heterogeneous vehicle fleet derived from the lateral-dynamics setup in~\cite{weber2026ifac}. All clients operate around $U=15\,\mathrm{m/s}$; speed is no longer used as a family-defining heterogeneity source. Three diagnostic generating families are used only for analysis and are never supplied to the algorithm:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{nominal}: unperturbed nominal vehicle parameters,
    \item \textbf{payload}: mass increased by $20\%$ and yaw inertia by $25\%$,
    \item \textbf{tire-degraded}: front cornering stiffness reduced by $25\%$.
\end{itemize}
Each family additionally contains the same small client-level parameter scatter. The true plant uses a smooth saturating lateral-tire law
\begin{equation}
    F_y=\mu F_z\tanh\!\left(\frac{C_\alpha\alpha}{\mu F_z}\right),
\end{equation}
while local identification and LQI synthesis retain the linear two-state $(\beta,r)$ model. This creates controlled structural mismatch while preserving the identification interface.

The steering-rate limit is set to a physically motivated $300^\circ/\mathrm{s}$ road-wheel rate and the sideslip feasibility bound is $|\beta|\le6^\circ$. The LQI search uses the normalized three-dimensional diagonal-$Q$ parameterization described in Sec.~\ref{sec:problem}.

\subsection{Phase-1 mechanism evidence}
The mechanism study uses common Sobol designs to compare identified dynamics distance, entire objective-landscape similarity, and direct controller-transfer loss. Transfer loss from donor $j$ to recipient $i$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
    L_{i\leftarrow j}=\frac{f_i(\xi_j^\star)-f_i(\xi_i^\star)}{f_i(\xi_i^\star)+\epsilon_J}.
    \label{eq:transfer_loss}
\end{equation}
On the final fixed-speed $N=60$ Phase-1C fleet, dynamics distance remains positively associated with transfer loss ($\rho=0.270$, 95\% CI $[0.165,0.384]$) and with whole-landscape distance ($\rho=0.341$, 95\% CI $[0.257,0.450]$). The donor--recipient analysis additionally shows directional transfer, motivating but not ultimately validating the recipient-aware extension.

\subsection{BO comparison}
The corrected medium-scale decision experiment uses 30 clients (10 per diagnostic family), five independent fleet/BO seeds, eight initial Sobol evaluations per client, and 12 subsequent EI-guided evaluations, for 20 local evaluations per client. Calibration observations drive the surrogate and incumbent selection; held-out test trajectories are used only for reporting. \draftnote{For the final campaign, use one fixed held-out test bank per seed/client across all methods and rounds.}

The primary metric is normalized simple regret versus local evaluation budget. The important interpretation is therefore not that federation changes the best attainable controller. Rather, selective sharing improves \emph{how quickly} clients reach the useful region of the LQI weight space. Global pooling can help very early, when observations are scarce, but becomes harmful once heterogeneous observations begin to distort the target client's surrogate. Dynamics similarity preserves the useful part of collaboration while reducing that negative transfer.

\subsection{The recipient-aware gate does not improve the main method}
Although Phase 1D showed a physically interpretable relation between recipient-predicted sideslip and directional transfer loss, the recipient-aware method does not outperform plain similarity weighting at any reported checkpoint. At the final medium-scale checkpoint its regret is $0.0181$, even worse than independent BO. The current evidence therefore does not justify the additional model-screening complexity. We retain the result as an informative ablation and carry only the similarity-weighted method into the final statistical campaign.

\subsection{Current limitations}
The medium-scale study contains only five independent fleet seeds. Individual clients within one seed share the same communication pool and are therefore not independent method-level replicates. The quantitative BO conclusions must consequently be confirmed with substantially more independent seeds before submission. The final campaign should use 15--20 seeds, extend the local evaluation budget to approximately 25 points, and report paired statistics at the fleet-seed level. AURC and family-specific results will test whether the sample-efficiency advantage persists across the full calibration trajectory and whether it is concentrated in particular plant families.

A second limitation is that scalar BO observations are shared across clients in the current implementation. The method keeps raw trajectories local, but does not provide formal privacy of objective values. Furthermore, the uncertainty-aware distance is symmetric even though cross-client controller transfer can be directional. The present results suggest that this limitation need not be corrected to obtain useful sample-efficiency gains, but more general directional descriptors remain an interesting extension.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
This paper studies collaborative LQI calibration in fleets whose clients share a control task but not identical dynamics. The proposed approach uses uncertain local system identifications as a continuous relevance descriptor for federated Bayesian optimization. Instead of globally pooling every calibration observation or assigning clients to hard clusters, each target client weights peer observations according to uncertainty-aware dynamics similarity and fits a target-specific GP surrogate for expected-improvement search.

The preliminary corrected medium-scale results support the intended mechanism: indiscriminate global sharing becomes harmful as calibration proceeds, whereas similarity-weighted FBO reaches near-oracle controller performance with fewer local evaluations than independent BO. A more elaborate recipient-aware directional gate does not improve the result and is therefore not retained as part of the main method. \draftnote{Replace this paragraph with the final 15--20-seed statistics and AURC result before submission.}
