\section{Detailed LQI Parameterization}

For yaw-rate tracking, the identified two-state model uses $\mathbf{x}=[\beta,r]^\top$ and is augmented by the integral tracking error
\begin{equation}
 z_{k+1}=z_k+T_s(r_k^{\rm ref}-r_k).
\end{equation}
With $\mathbf{x}_k^a=[\mathbf{x}_k^\top,z_k]^\top$, the fixed scale vector is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{s}=[0.053229,\,0.575573,\,0.139862]^\top,
\end{equation}
and the LQI penalties are
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf Q(\boldsymbol{\xi})
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
 \frac{10^{\xi_1}}{s_1^2},
 \frac{10^{\xi_2}}{s_2^2},
 \frac{10^{\xi_3}}{s_3^2}\right),
 \qquad \mathbf R=1.
\end{equation}
The scales were fixed before the confirmatory BO study from representative nominal closed-loop trajectories and were not tuned per vehicle family.

At target evaluation $n$, expected improvement is maximized over the fixed 256-point Sobol candidate set. For dynamics-weighted transfer, source-record noise is scaled as
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{ij}=\frac{\alpha_0}{\max(s_{ij},0.02)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{RGPE transfer baseline.}
RGPE was added after the original confirmatory analysis as a literature comparator; it reuses the frozen 20 fleet realizations, mature source histories, target systems, local budget, candidate set, and dense-reference scoring protocol. It uses the same 25 historical evaluations per source and the same target evaluation budget, but fits one task-standardized GP per source system rather than pooling the observations. All RGPE task GPs use the same constant-times-Mat\'ern-$5/2$ kernel family, initial length scale, and length-scale bounds as the pooled surrogates in Table~S1. A target GP is refit after each local evaluation. At every BO round, 256 joint posterior samples are used to compute pairwise ranking loss on the observed target points; the target model is scored by leave-one-out prediction using its fitted hyperparameters. A model's ensemble weight is the fraction of posterior samples for which it attains the minimum ranking loss. Following the RGPE dilution safeguard, a source is discarded when its median sampled loss exceeds the 95th percentile of target-model loss. The ensemble moments are
\begin{equation}
 \mu_{\rm RGPE}(\boldsymbol\xi)=\sum_q w_q\mu_q(\boldsymbol\xi),
 \qquad
 \sigma_{\rm RGPE}^2(\boldsymbol\xi)=\sum_q w_q^2\sigma_q^2(\boldsymbol\xi),
\end{equation}
and expected improvement is evaluated on the same 256-point candidate set as the other methods.

\section{Vehicle Model, Fleet Generation, and Identification}

The continuous-time linear control-design model is
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf A\mathbf x+\mathbf B\delta,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{A}=\begin{bmatrix}
-\dfrac{C_f+C_r}{mU} & \dfrac{-aC_f+bC_r}{mU^2}-1\\[2mm]
\dfrac{-aC_f+bC_r}{I_z} & -\dfrac{a^2C_f+b^2C_r}{I_zU}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf{B}=\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac{C_f}{mU}\\[2mm]\dfrac{aC_f}{I_z}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The model is discretized by zero-order hold with $T_s=0.05$\,s.

Controller candidates are evaluated on a nonlinear lateral plant. The slip angles are
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_f=\delta-\beta-\frac{ar}{U},
 \qquad
 \alpha_r=-\beta+\frac{br}{U},
\end{equation}
and the tire forces are
\begin{equation}
 F_{y,q}=\mu F_{z,q}\tanh\!\left(\frac{C_q\alpha_q}{\mu F_{z,q}}\right),
 \qquad q\in\{f,r\},
\end{equation}
with static axle loads $F_{z,f}=mgb/(a+b)$ and $F_{z,r}=mga/(a+b)$. The nonlinear states evolve as
\begin{equation}
 \dot\beta=\frac{F_{y,f}+F_{y,r}}{mU}-r,
 \qquad
 \dot r=\frac{aF_{y,f}-bF_{y,r}}{I_z}.
\end{equation}

For each generated system, $m$, $I_z$, $C_f$, $C_r$, and $U$ are independently multiplied by $1+\eta$, $\eta\sim\mathcal U[-0.025,0.025]$, while $a$ and $b$ remain fixed. A system is resampled if the fixed baseline controller is infeasible on the calibration task, with at most 50 attempts.

The local discrete model is estimated as
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{\Theta}_i=[\mathbf A_{d,i}\;\mathbf B_{d,i}]\in\mathbb R^{2\times3},
 \qquad
 \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i=\operatorname{vec}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_i)\in\mathbb R^6,
\end{equation}
using matrix RLS with regressor $[\mathbf x_k^\top,u_k]^\top$, nominal-model initialization, forgetting factor $\lambda=0.99$, and initial covariance $\mathbf P_0=\mathbf I_3$. Each system processes eight repeated 6\,s identification episodes. If $\mathbf D_i=\operatorname{diag}(\hat\sigma_{i,1}^2,\hat\sigma_{i,2}^2)$ contains the final row-wise innovation variances and $\mathbf P_i$ is the regressor covariance, the precision used for dynamics distance is
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\mathbf W}_i=\operatorname{pinv}\!\left(\mathbf D_i\otimes\mathbf P_i\right).
\end{equation}

\section{Calibration Task and Held-Out Protocol}

Every candidate is evaluated on three stochastic episodes for each of two lane-change half-durations, $T_{\rm lc}\in\{1,2\}$\,s. Episode seeds are generated deterministically per client and maneuver, so all systems share the same maneuver distribution but not identical noise realizations. The 128-point calibration-landscape study uses this same six-episode calibration objective at every shared Sobol coordinate. For a trajectory with $T$ samples,
\begin{equation}
 J_i(\boldsymbol{\xi},\omega)
 =\sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{T}
 \left(r_{i,k}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\omega)-r_k^{\rm ref}\right)^2}.
\end{equation}
The unfiltered reference is zero until $T_0=1$\,s, follows a positive sine lobe of duration $T_{\rm lc}$ and then a negative sine lobe of equal duration, with peak $r_{\max}=35^\circ/$s. A zero-phase first-order Butterworth filter with 1\,Hz cutoff smooths the waveform. Each episode lasts 6\,s. Feasibility requires successful LQI synthesis, $|\beta|\le6^\circ$, and steering-rate change no larger than $0.2618$\,rad per simulation step.

\section{Offline-Reference Convergence Audit}

The offline reference is used only for reporting and never enters GP fitting, acquisition, objective normalization, or infeasibility handling. Its global-search stage uses 1024 Sobol points before eight bounded Powell refinements, and the resulting best candidate is scored on an independent reference-test bank with 20 stochastic episodes per maneuver. To check whether the 1024-point global search is sufficiently dense, we performed a nested Sobol convergence audit on one target from each vehicle family using prefixes of a common 4096-point design. The best 1024-point Sobol value differed from the corresponding 4096-point value by only 0.018\% on average. This supports numerical convergence of the search resolution used in the final reference computation, while not constituting a proof of global optimality.

\section{Additional Result Diagnostics}

\paragraph{RGPE adaptation.}
Across the 120 held-out targets, the mean target-model weight is 0.703 at $n=3$, decreases to 0.076 at $n=5$, and rises to 0.124 by $n=12$. The mean number of source models retained by the 95th-percentile dilution guard decreases from 28.9 at $n=3$ to 20.5 at $n=12$. Ensemble-weight entropy rises from 1.54 at $n=3$ to approximately 3.06 at $n=5$ before declining to 2.50 at $n=12$. Thus RGPE is not equivalent to uniform averaging: it redistributes weight strongly and becomes more selective as target evidence accumulates. Nevertheless, its dense-reference restricted mean is $N_{5\%}=4.625$, compared with 6.717 for independent BO and 3.083 for both pooled warm starts. Its paired difference relative to global pooling is $+1.542$ evaluations with 95\% CI $(0.92,2.28)$ and Wilcoxon $p=0.0009$.
