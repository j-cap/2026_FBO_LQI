\begin{abstract}
Controller calibration is often repeated from scratch for every new system variant, even when previously commissioned systems have already explored the same controller-weight space. We study when such historical black-box evaluations transfer across heterogeneous dynamical systems. For LQI yaw-rate control, normalized source-system observations warm-start a Gaussian-process surrogate while model identification and gain synthesis remain target-specific. Across 120 held-out target-calibration problems, fleet-informed Bayesian optimization reduces the budget-restricted mean number of target evaluations required to reach within 5\% of an independently scored reference from 6.72 to 3.08, a reduction of 54\%. A post-hoc ranking-weighted transfer-BO baseline also benefits from source data but requires 4.63 evaluations on average. Ten historical evaluations per source recover about 93\% of the mature-fleet experiment saving. Although the underlying vehicle dynamics differ substantially, 2174 of 2175 system pairs retain calibration-landscape rank correlation $\rho_{ij}^{J}\ge0.95$ on jointly feasible points; correspondingly, dynamics-aware source weighting provides no measurable advantage over simple pooling. The results show that task-level calibration landscapes can be substantially more transferable than the underlying plant dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Bayesian optimization, controller calibration, transfer learning, data-efficient control, heterogeneous dynamical systems, LQI
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
Controller calibration is an important but expensive part of commissioning modern dynamical systems. Even when the controller structure follows from model-based design, parameters such as linear-quadratic regulator or integral-control weights determine the final compromise between tracking performance, control effort, and robustness. Bayesian optimization (BO) is attractive for this setting because it can optimize low-dimensional controller parameters from a limited number of expensive closed-loop evaluations~\citep{frazier2018tutorial,marco2016lqr,berkenkamp2016safe,schillinger2017safe,vonrohr2024local}.

A fleet of related systems changes this optimization problem. Vehicles or machines are commissioned repeatedly, so previously calibrated systems have already evaluated controller parameters that a newly arriving system would otherwise have to rediscover. Reusing these observations can reduce target-system experimentation, but only if the corresponding control-performance landscapes are sufficiently aligned. Plant heterogeneity therefore creates a transfer question that is distinct from ordinary single-system BO: \emph{when are controller-calibration observations from one dynamical system informative for another?}

This question is nontrivial because the same calibration vector does not represent the same feedback gain on every plant. In the setting studied here, each system identifies its own control-design model and recomputes an LQI gain for every candidate weight vector. What is shared across systems is only the low-dimensional calibration coordinate and its observed normalized performance. Hence successful transfer requires similarity of the induced calibration objectives, not equality of the underlying dynamics or controllers.

\subsection{Related work and positioning}
BO has been used for automatic controller tuning in LQR, PI, and safety-constrained calibration problems~\citep{marco2016lqr,berkenkamp2016safe,schillinger2017safe,vonrohr2024local}. Transfer-learning BO reuses observations or surrogate information from related optimization tasks~\citep{joy2019transfer,tighineanu2022transfer,perrone2018scalable}; ranking-weighted GP ensembles (RGPE) combine source-task surrogates according to their agreement with target-side rankings~\citep{feurer2018rgpe}. Federated and collaborative BO consider optimization with information distributed across agents~\citep{dai2020fts,dai2021dpfts,chen2025collaborative}, while federated control and identification exchange model or design information across heterogeneous dynamical systems~\citep{wang2026fedlqr,weber2026ifac}.

Our objective is different from proposing a new acquisition function, a common fleet controller, or a distributed GP algorithm. We deliberately use unstructured pooling as the lowest-complexity transfer mechanism: if it already succeeds, the gain can be attributed to cross-system alignment of the calibration objective rather than to a specialized multi-task surrogate. Conversely, failure of pooling would motivate more structured transfer models. A dynamics-weighted variant then tests the natural hypothesis that observations from physically similar systems should be trusted more strongly. This design makes global pooling a diagnostic transfer baseline and lets the experiments distinguish \emph{plant heterogeneity} from \emph{calibration-landscape heterogeneity}, which is the central scientific question of the paper. Because the transferable records are compact and do not contain raw trajectories, the same formulation is compatible with distributed data ownership, but privacy mechanisms and distributed surrogate training are not contributions of this work.

The answer need not favor selective transfer. Different physical plants can induce similar rankings of the same low-dimensional LQI weight configurations even when their identified dynamics are clearly separated. If so, broad pooling should already provide effective surrogate coverage and dynamics-aware attenuation may add little. Conversely, if calibration landscapes change strongly with the plant, unweighted historical data can create negative transfer.

We evaluate these alternatives on heterogeneous nonlinear vehicle lateral dynamics. Previously calibrated source systems provide normalized controller-calibration observations. A new target begins with two local initialization experiments, combines them with the source observations, and sequentially evaluates new LQI calibrations. Only experiments executed on the target count toward its incremental commissioning cost; every candidate gain is synthesized from the target's locally identified model.

Figure~\ref{fig:concept} summarizes the cross-system calibration workflow.

The original held-out protocol---three primary methods, local experiment budget, metric, and statistical tests---was fixed before the 20 final fleet realizations were examined. RGPE was added subsequently as a literature transfer-BO comparator and evaluated only on the same frozen fleet realizations, source histories, targets, target budget, and reference-scoring protocol. The contributions are:
\begin{samepage}
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate \emph{fleet-informed controller calibration} as transfer between related black-box control-optimization tasks. Source observations are normalized in a common three-dimensional controller-weight space, while model identification and feedback-gain synthesis remain system-specific. The evaluation explicitly separates historical source information from newly executed target experiments.
    \item In 120 held-out target-calibration problems, global pooling reduces the budget-restricted mean target effort from 6.72 to 3.08 evaluations and, in a post-hoc comparison, outperforms the RGPE transfer-BO baseline at 4.63 evaluations. A source-maturity study shows that ten historical evaluations per source recover about 93\% of the mean experiment saving obtained with 25 evaluations per source.
    \item We characterize the mechanism behind this transfer. Despite substantial plant heterogeneity, 2174 of 2175 system pairs satisfy $\rho_{ij}^{J}\ge0.95$ on jointly feasible calibration points. Dynamics similarity retains a modest residual association with landscape similarity, yet dynamics-aware weighting provides no measurable improvement over unweighted pooling.
\end{itemize}
\end{samepage}

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{System-specific controller calibration}
Consider heterogeneous systems $i\in\mathcal C$ with nonlinear dynamics
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{x}_{i,k+1}=\mathbf{f}_i(\mathbf{x}_{i,k},u_{i,k},\mathbf{w}_{i,k}),
 \qquad
 \mathbf{y}_{i,k}=\mathbf{h}_i(\mathbf{x}_{i,k},\mathbf{v}_{i,k}),
 \label{eq:true_plant_generic}
\end{equation}
and a locally identified linear control-design model
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{x}_{k+1}=\hat{\mathbf A}_i\mathbf{x}_k+\hat{\mathbf B}_i u_k.
 \label{eq:identified_model}
\end{equation}
For the vehicle study, $\mathbf{x}=[\beta,r]^\top$ contains sideslip and yaw rate. Yaw-rate tracking is augmented by integral action, and the LQI state penalty is parameterized by $\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Xi\subset\mathbb R^3$ as
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf Q(\boldsymbol{\xi})
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(10^{\xi_1}/s_1^2,\,10^{\xi_2}/s_2^2,\,10^{\xi_3}/s_3^2\right),
 \qquad \mathbf R=1,
 \label{eq:lqi_weights}
\end{equation}
where the fixed channel scales $\mathbf s$ are shared across all systems. Every calibration vector is converted into a target-specific gain,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf K_i(\boldsymbol{\xi})
 =\operatorname{LQI}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf A}_i,\hat{\mathbf B}_i,\hat{\mathbf C}_i,
 \mathbf Q(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\mathbf R\right).
 \label{eq:lqi_gain}
\end{equation}
Thus the same $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ can produce different gains on different plants. The transferable object is the observed performance of points in the common calibration space, not the feedback gain itself. Full LQI scaling and vehicle-model details are provided in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Transferable calibration records and evaluation metric}
A previously calibrated source system $j$ contributes normalized task-level observations
\begin{equation}
 \calD_j^{\rm hist}
 =\left\{\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j,m},\tilde y_{j,m}\right)\right\}_{m=1}^{M_j},
 \qquad
 \tilde y_{j,m}=\frac{J_j(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j,m})}{J_j^{\rm base}},
 \label{eq:historical_data}
\end{equation}
where $J_j^{\rm base}$ is the cost of a fixed baseline controller. Each record contains only the three calibration coordinates and one normalized scalar cost. Historical records belonging to the target itself are excluded.

The paper reports incremental target-system effort. Let $J_i^{\rm ref}$ denote an independently computed system-specific offline reference. It is obtained from 1024 Sobol candidates followed by bounded local refinement and is scored on an independent reference-test bank; it never enters GP fitting or acquisition. If $J_{i,\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{best}}(n)$ is the best independently rescored incumbent after $n$ target evaluations, the 5\% hitting time is
\begin{equation}
 T_{i,5\%}
 =\min\left\{n:J_{i,\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{best}}(n)\le1.05J_i^{\rm ref}\right\}.
 \label{eq:n5}
\end{equation}
All runs share the budget $B=12$. We report the restricted mean $N_{i,5\%}=\min(T_{i,5\%},B)$ and treat unresolved runs as right-censored in success summaries.

\section{Fleet-Informed Bayesian Optimization}

At local evaluation $n$, the target optimizer fits one GP to its own observations and the available source histories,
\begin{equation}
 \calD_i^{(n)}
 =\calD_i^{\rm local}(n)\cup\bigcup_{j\ne i}\calD_j^{\rm hist}.
 \label{eq:pooled_dataset}
\end{equation}
The GP and expected-improvement acquisition rule are otherwise unchanged from ordinary BO. This deliberately simple full-pooling baseline isolates the value of cross-system calibration information rather than introducing a new acquisition function or task kernel. It therefore tests whether transfer is already explained by objective alignment before introducing more expressive multi-task BO machinery. The search space is $\Xi=[-3,3]^3$, and every target begins with the same two-point Sobol initialization. Detailed GP settings are listed in the supplementary material.

The pre-specified comparison contains three methods. \emph{Independent BO} uses only target observations. \emph{Global warm start} assigns all source records unit weight. \emph{Dynamics-weighted warm start} tests whether physically similar sources should be trusted more strongly. We additionally evaluate \emph{RGPE}~\citep{feurer2018rgpe} post hoc as an established transfer-BO comparator, using the frozen confirmatory source histories and target protocol. RGPE fits one GP per historical source task and one target GP, then weights their predictions according to posterior-sampled ranking loss on the observed target points. Local RLS identification yields the model vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i$ and precision $\hat{\mathbf W}_i$; without using family labels, we define
\begin{align}
 (\distdyn_{ij})^2
 &=\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_j\right)^\top
 \frac{\hat{\mathbf W}_i+\hat{\mathbf W}_j}{2}
 \left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_j\right), \nonumber\\
 s_{ij}&=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\distdyn_{ij})^2}{2\ell_d^2}\right),
 \qquad
 \alpha_{ij}=\frac{\alpha_0}{\max(s_{ij},0.02)},
 \label{eq:dyn_weighting}
\end{align}
where $\ell_d$ is the median pairwise-distance bandwidth and $\alpha_{ij}$ is the source-record GP noise. If dynamics similarity predicts transfer usefulness, this weighting should outperform global pooling.

\section{Experimental Design}

\paragraph{Vehicle benchmark and local identification.}
The benchmark uses a two-state lateral bicycle model for local LQI synthesis and a nonlinear tire-force plant for closed-loop evaluation, creating deliberate model mismatch. Each realization contains 30 systems from three families: nominal; payload, with $m$ increased by 20\% and $I_z$ by 25\%; and tire-degraded, with $C_f$ reduced by 25\%. Within each family, $m$, $I_z$, $C_f$, $C_r$, and constant rollout speed $U$ receive independent $\pm2.5\%$ multiplicative scatter. Family labels are never provided to BO. Each system locally identifies its discrete model by matrix RLS over eight 6\,s closed-loop episodes with forgetting factor $0.99$ and no added open-loop excitation. Full plant, estimator, and feasibility equations are given in the supplementary material.

\paragraph{Calibration task.}
Every BO query averages yaw-rate RMSE over three stochastic episodes of each of two lane-change maneuvers with $T_{\rm lc}\in\{1,2\}$\,s. Candidates are infeasible if LQI synthesis fails, $|\beta|>6^\circ$, or the steering-rate change exceeds $0.2618$\,rad per simulation step. The same task distribution and constraints are used for source histories, target calibration, and the offline reference.

\paragraph{Held-out evaluation.}
The confirmatory study uses 20 fleet realizations not used for development of the three pre-specified methods. In each realization, every system first contributes a 25-evaluation independent-BO history. Six systems, two per family, are then treated in turn as new targets; their own histories are removed, leaving $29\times25=725$ source records. Each target receives two local Sobol evaluations and at most ten BO-selected evaluations, producing 120 target-calibration problems. The post-hoc RGPE comparison reuses these exact frozen histories, targets, and budgets without altering the primary methods. Fleet realization is the statistical replication unit. Paired method differences are computed after averaging the six targets within each realization; 95\% intervals use 10,000 bootstrap resamples, with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the same 20 realization-level differences. A nested source-maturity study exposes $M_{\rm hist}\in\{2,5,10,25\}$ records per source while keeping the target protocol fixed.

\paragraph{Calibration-landscape analysis.}
To test whether plant heterogeneity induces task heterogeneity, all 30 systems in each of five independent fleet realizations are evaluated on one fixed 128-point Sobol design shared across all systems and realizations. Each grid point is scored with the same six-episode calibration objective used by BO: three stochastic noise realizations for each of the two lane-change maneuvers. The maneuver distribution is common across systems, while the deterministic episode seeds are client-specific. For each pair, rank correlation is computed only on jointly feasible calibration points,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{ij}^{J}
 =\operatorname{Spearman}\!\left(
 \{J_i(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(m)})\}_{m\in\mathcal F_{ij}},
 \{J_j(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(m)})\}_{m\in\mathcal F_{ij}}
 \right),
 \label{eq:landscape_similarity}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_{ij}$ is the jointly feasible index set. The study contains 2175 within-realization system pairs. These pairs are not treated as independent replicates: realization-level summaries are computed within each of the five fleets, and confidence intervals are obtained by bootstrapping across fleet realizations.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Fleet warm start halves target-system experimentation}
Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} shows independently rescored reference-relative regret over the local budget. All transfer methods improve over independent BO, with the two pooled warm starts converging fastest.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\figurepdf{0.88\textwidth}{figures/fig3_confirmatory_convergence.pdf}
\caption{Held-out convergence over 20 fleet realizations and 120 target-calibration problems. Curves show the median independently rescored reference-relative regret; shaded bands show the interquartile range across target problems. RGPE transfers useful source information, while global and dynamics-weighted pooling converge fastest.}
\label{fig:convergence}
\end{figure}

Independent BO has a budget-restricted mean $N_{5\%}$ of 6.717 evaluations, compared with 3.083 for global warm start, a 54.1\% reduction. The paired realization-level difference is $-3.633$ evaluations with 95\% bootstrap CI $(-5.01,-2.37)$ and Wilcoxon $p=0.0005$. In the additional post-hoc comparison, RGPE also improves over independent BO, reaching $N_{5\%}=4.625$ with paired difference $-2.092$ evaluations, 95\% CI $(-3.52,-0.69)$, and $p=0.0151$. However, RGPE remains 1.542 evaluations slower than global pooling, with 95\% CI $(0.92,2.28)$ and $p=0.0009$. Independent BO reaches the 5\% criterion within budget for 95 of 120 targets (79.2\%), RGPE for 118 of 120 (98.3\%), and both pooled warm starts for all 120. Detailed threshold-attainment curves are reported in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Moderate source maturity captures most of the benefit}
Figure~\ref{fig:maturity} varies the amount of historical experience available from each source. With $M_{\rm hist}=2$, the restricted mean $N_{5\%}=6.475$ remains close to independent BO. It decreases to 5.667 for $M_{\rm hist}=5$ and to 3.525 for $M_{\rm hist}=10$, where all 120 targets reach the criterion within budget. At $M_{\rm hist}=25$, the restricted mean is 3.283.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\figurepdf{0.58\textwidth}{figures/fig7a_fleet_maturity_absolute.pdf}
\caption{Target-system sample efficiency versus source-fleet maturity. Historical source records are nested prefixes of independent-BO trajectories. Error bars use fleet realization as the resampling unit; the top axis reports the total number of source records available to a target.}
\label{fig:maturity}
\end{figure}

Ten evaluations per source recover approximately 93\% of the mean experiment saving obtained with 25 evaluations while using only 40\% of the per-source historical budget. The statistically resolved gain therefore appears between five and ten evaluations per source; deeper histories mainly improve the remaining tail of slower target calibrations. The $M_{\rm hist}=25$ sensitivity endpoint differs slightly from the main mature-fleet result because the nested study uses a common two-point source initialization rather than the main eight-point mature-history initialization.

\subsection{Calibration landscapes explain why simple pooling is sufficient}
The key mechanistic result is that substantial plant heterogeneity produces only limited calibration-landscape heterogeneity. Figure~\ref{fig:landscape} compares physical variation with pairwise calibration-landscape alignment.

Across all 2175 pairs, 2174 pairs (99.95\%) satisfy $\rho_{ij}^{J}\ge0.95$, with a minimum of 0.9496. Mean correlation is 0.992 for same-family pairs and 0.987 for cross-family pairs. Dynamics similarity still has a modest association with landscape similarity: the median within-realization Spearman correlation is 0.245 with 95\% CI $(0.162,0.288)$. The objectives are therefore not identical, but their ordering is highly conserved.

Consistent with this structure, the tested dynamics-aware weighting in~\eqref{eq:dyn_weighting} provides no measurable improvement over global pooling. The paired realization-level difference in restricted $N_{5\%}$ is $0.000$ evaluations with 95\% CI $(-0.05,+0.04)$ and Wilcoxon $p=1.000$. This null result applies to the present precision-weighted distance and GP-noise attenuation, not to every possible task-aware transfer model.

A separate nine-source coverage study, reported in the supplementary material, confirms that this conclusion does not depend on access to all 29 peers or on a particular same-family source composition.

\section{Discussion}
The experiments support a distinction between \emph{plant heterogeneity} and \emph{task-landscape heterogeneity}. The vehicle families are physically distinguishable, yet their LQI calibration rankings remain strongly aligned. In this regime, broad historical coverage is more valuable than either the tested dynamics-based attenuation or RGPE's target-adaptive source weighting. RGPE confirms useful structured transfer: it significantly outperforms independent BO but remains 1.54 evaluations slower than global pooling. Its weights redistribute strongly and prune more sources as target data accumulate, consistent with spending scarce early target information distinguishing sources whose calibration landscapes are already highly aligned. More heterogeneous task distributions may instead favor selective or multi-task transfer.

The source-maturity result qualifies this conclusion. Two shared initialization points provide little benefit because they repeatedly sample the same locations across the fleet. Sequential source BO diversifies the historical coverage, and the major improvement appears between five and ten evaluations per source. Thus prior fleet experience is useful only after it has explored enough of the shared calibration space; beyond that point, deeper histories mainly reduce tail risk.

The transferable information object is the task-level record $(\boldsymbol{\xi},\tilde y)$ rather than a plant model or feedback gain. At $M_{\rm hist}=10$, a target receives 290 such four-scalar records from 29 sources. This is compact relative to raw time-series data, but the reported 54\% reduction concerns \emph{incremental target-system experimentation}; the historical evaluations were generated during earlier commissioning and are not free.

Several limitations remain. Validation is simulation based, and the study considers one three-dimensional LQI parameterization, one lateral-control task, and a narrow constant-speed operating regime. Higher-dimensional controller classes or more heterogeneous task distributions may exhibit weaker landscape alignment and may favor selective or multi-task BO models. The dynamics-aware ablation uses one fixed distance-to-noise mapping (median distance bandwidth and a 0.02 weighting floor); we do not claim that its null result covers all dynamics-informed transfer schemes. The offline reference is numerically well converged but is not a proof of the global optimum. Finally, the work characterizes transferability and sample efficiency rather than privacy or communication-optimal distributed BO.

\section{Conclusion}
Fleet-informed BO reuses normalized controller-calibration observations across heterogeneous dynamical systems while retaining target-specific model identification and gain synthesis. Across 120 held-out vehicle calibration problems, this reduces the budget-restricted mean target effort from 6.72 to 3.08 evaluations. Ten historical evaluations per source recover about 93\% of the mature-fleet saving. In a post-hoc comparison, simple pooling also outperforms the RGPE transfer-BO baseline. The mechanism is unusually strong task alignment: 2174 of 2175 system pairs satisfy $\rho_{ij}^{J}\ge0.95$ despite substantial plant heterogeneity, explaining why broad pooling is effective in the studied regime.
