\begin{abstract}
Controller calibration is often repeated from scratch for every new system variant, even when previously commissioned systems have already explored the same controller-weight space. We study when such historical black-box evaluations transfer across heterogeneous dynamical systems. For LQI yaw-rate control of heterogeneous vehicle lateral dynamics, normalized source-system observations warm-start a Gaussian-process surrogate while model identification and gain synthesis remain target-specific. 
\JWrev{Across 120 held-out target-calibration problems, fleet-informed Bayesian optimization reduces the mean number of target evaluations needed to reach within 5\% of the reference controller's tracking cost from 6.72 to 3.08, a 54\% reduction, with counts capped at the 12-evaluation budget.} 
A post-hoc ranking-weighted transfer-BO baseline also benefits from source data but requires 4.63 evaluations on average. With ten historical evaluations available from each source, fleet-informed BO recovers about 93\% of the reduction in target-system experiments obtained with 25 evaluations per source. A post-hoc landscape diagnostic finds that, despite the differing vehicle dynamics, \MGrev{2174 of 2175 system pairs have calibration-cost Spearman correlations of at least 0.95 on jointly feasible points.} Correspondingly, dynamics-aware source weighting provides no measurable advantage over simple pooling. The results show that task-level calibration landscapes can be substantially more transferable than the underlying plant dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Bayesian optimization, controller calibration, transfer learning, data-efficient control, heterogeneous dynamical systems, LQI
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
Controller calibration is an important but expensive part of commissioning modern dynamical systems. 
Even when the controller structure follows from model-based design, parameters such as linear-quadratic regulator or integral-control weights determine the final compromise between tracking performance, control effort, and robustness. 
Bayesian optimization (BO) is attractive for this setting because it can optimize low-dimensional controller parameters from a limited number of expensive closed-loop evaluations~\citep{frazier2018tutorial,marco2016lqr,berkenkamp2016safe,schillinger2017safe,vonrohr2024local}.

A fleet of related systems alters this optimization problem. 
Vehicles or machines are commissioned repeatedly, so previously calibrated systems have already evaluated controller parameters that a newly arriving system would otherwise have to rediscover. 
Reusing these observations can reduce target-system experimentation, but only if the corresponding control-performance landscapes are sufficiently aligned.
Plant heterogeneity therefore creates a transfer question that is distinct from ordinary single-system BO: \emph{when are controller-calibration observations from one dynamical system informative for another?}

\JWrev{We investigate this question by transferring LQI design weights while retaining system-specific controller synthesis. Each system identifies its own model and recomputes the feedback gain for every candidate weight vector, yielding personalized controllers even when design weights are shared.}
\MGnew{Systems share only the three parameters defining their LQI design weights and the resulting normalized tracking error.}
Hence successful transfer requires similarity of the induced calibration objectives, not equality of the underlying dynamics or controllers.

\subsection{Related work and positioning}
BO has been used for automatic controller tuning in LQR, PI, and safety-constrained calibration problems~\citep{marco2016lqr,berkenkamp2016safe,schillinger2017safe,vonrohr2024local}. 
Transfer-learning BO reuses observations or surrogate information from related optimization tasks~\citep{joy2019transfer,tighineanu2022transfer,perrone2018scalable}. 
Multi-task BO jointly models related tasks with structured Gaussian processes, while ranking-weighted GP ensembles (RGPE) weight source-task surrogates by agreement with target rankings.~\citep{feurer2018rgpe}. 
Federated and collaborative BO consider optimization with information distributed across agents~\citep{dai2020fts,dai2021dpfts,chen2025collaborative}, while federated control and identification exchange model or design information across heterogeneous dynamical systems~\citep{wang2026fedlqr,weber2026ifac}.

\JWrev{These approaches motivate a complementary question: does plant heterogeneity require selective transfer of controller-calibration observations? Different plants may rank the same design weights similarly, making broad pooling effective, whereas conflicting rankings can cause negative transfer. We therefore compare simple pooling with dynamics-based source weighting to assess whether physical similarity improves transfer. Our focus is the reuse of calibration observations, with privacy mechanisms and distributed surrogate training outside the scope.}

\JWrev{We examine this question on heterogeneous nonlinear vehicle lateral dynamics using the workflow in Figure~\ref{fig:concept}. Each target begins with two local evaluations and uses normalized source observations to guide further LQI calibration. We measure the additional target experiments required, treating source histories as prior commissioning experience. The primary comparison was fixed before evaluating 20 held-out fleets. RGPE was added subsequently as an exploratory comparator using the same histories, targets, and evaluation protocol.}

\JWrev{This study makes three contributions:}
\begin{samepage}
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate fleet-informed calibration of personalized controllers through normalized observations in a shared three-dimensional weight space, separating historical source evaluations from new target experiments. 
    \item Across 120 held-out calibration problems, pooling reduces budget-restricted mean target effort from 6.72 to 3.08 evaluations, compared with 4.63 for post-hoc RGPE. Ten historical evaluations per source recover 93\% of the saving obtained with 25.
    \item A post-hoc diagnostic finds calibration-cost Spearman correlations of at least \MGrev{0.95 for 2174 of 2175 system pairs on jointly feasible points, despite plant heterogeneity}. 
    Dynamics-aware weighting provides no measurable improvement over pooling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{System-specific controller calibration}
Consider heterogeneous systems $i\in\mathcal C$ with nonlinear dynamics in state-space representation
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{x}_{i,k+1}=\mathbf{f}_i(\mathbf{x}_{i,k},u_{i,k},\mathbf{w}_{i,k}),
 \qquad
 \mathbf{y}_{i,k}=\mathbf{h}_i(\mathbf{x}_{i,k},\mathbf{v}_{i,k}),
 \label{eq:true_plant_generic}
\end{equation}
\JWrev{where $\mathbf w_{i,k}$ and $\mathbf v_{i,k}$ denote process disturbances and measurement noise, respectively. A linear control-design model is identified separately for each system,}
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{x}_{k+1}=\hat{\mathbf A}_i\mathbf{x}_k+\hat{\mathbf B}_i u_k.
 \label{eq:identified_model}
\end{equation}
For the vehicle study, the state $\mathbf{x}=[\beta,r]^\top$ contains sideslip and yaw rate. Yaw-rate tracking is augmented by integral action, and the LQI state penalty is parameterized by $\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Xi\subset\mathbb R^3$ as
\begin{equation}
 \color{JWgreen}
 \mathbf Q(\boldsymbol{\xi})
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{10^{\xi_1}}{s_1^2},\frac{10^{\xi_2}}{s_2^2},\frac{10^{\xi_3}}{s_3^2}\right),
 \qquad \mathbf R=1,
 \label{eq:lqi_weights}
\end{equation}
where the fixed channel scales $s_1$, $s_2$, and $s_3$ are shared across all systems. Every calibration vector $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ is converted into a target-specific gain,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf K_i(\boldsymbol{\xi})
 =\operatorname{LQI}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf A}_i,\hat{\mathbf B}_i,\hat{\mathbf C}_i,
 \mathbf Q(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\mathbf R\right).
 \label{eq:lqi_gain}
\end{equation}
Thus the same $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ can produce different gains on different plants. \MGrev{Shared observations relate LQI design weights to normalized closed-loop tracking RMSE, rather than to the quadratic LQI design cost.} \MGnew{Supplementary Sections 1 and 4 detail the weight parameterization and evaluation episodes, and Section 6 reports additional landscape comparisons.}

\subsection{Transferable calibration records and evaluation metric}
A previously calibrated source system $j$ contributes normalized task-level observations
\begin{equation}
 \calD_j^{\rm hist}
 =\left\{\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j,m},\tilde y_{j,m}\right)\right\}_{m=1}^{M_j},
 \qquad
 \tilde y_{j,m}=\frac{J_j(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j,m})}{J_j^{\rm base}},
 \label{eq:historical_data}
\end{equation}
\MGnew{where $J_j^{\rm base}$ is the cost under the fixed baseline controller. For system $i$, the tracking cost averages the yaw-rate RMSE over $E$ episodes,} {\color{MGpurple}
\begin{equation} 
    J_i(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\frac{1}{E}\sum_{e=1}^{E}\sqrt{\frac{1}{K_e}\sum_{k=1}^{K_e}\left(r_{i,k}^{(e)}(\boldsymbol{\xi})-r_{\mathrm{ref},k}^{(e)}\right)^2}. 
\label{eq:tracking_cost}
\end{equation}}
%
\MGnew{Here $K_e$ is the number of samples in episode $e$, and $r_{i,k}^{(e)}$ and $r_{\mathrm{ref},k}^{(e)}$ are the measured and reference yaw rates.} \JWrev{Each record contains only the three calibration coordinates and the normalized scalar cost $\tilde y_{j,m}$ defined in~\eqref{eq:historical_data}.} Historical records belonging to the target itself are excluded.

The paper reports incremental target-system effort. \JWrev{Let $J_i^{\rm ref}$ denote the tracking cost of a system-specific reference controller computed separately for evaluation.} It is obtained from 1024 Sobol candidates followed by bounded local refinement and is scored on an independent reference-test bank. It never enters GP fitting or acquisition. \JWrev{After each target evaluation, the controller with the lowest observed calibration cost is evaluated on a separate test bank shared with the reference controller. These test scores do not guide BO. Let $J_{i,\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{best}}(n)$ denote the lowest test cost among these controllers through evaluation $n$. The reference-relative regret is} {\color{MGpurple}\begin{equation} r_i^{\mathrm{ref}}(n)=\frac{J_{i,\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{best}}(n)-J_i^{\rm ref}}{J_i^{\rm ref}}. \label{eq:reference_regret}\end{equation}} \MGnew{The 5\% hitting time is}
\begin{equation}
 T_{i,5\%}
 =\min\left\{n:J_{i,\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{best}}(n)\le1.05J_i^{\rm ref}\right\}.
 \label{eq:n5}
\end{equation}
All target-calibration runs share a maximum local budget of $B=12$ target-system evaluations, comprising two Sobol initialization evaluations and at most ten BO-selected evaluations. We report the restricted mean $N_{i,5\%}=\min(T_{i,5\%},B)$ and treat unresolved runs as right-censored in success summaries.

\section{Fleet-Informed Bayesian Optimization}

At local evaluation $n$, the target optimizer fits one GP to \JWrev{its local observations $\calD_i^{\rm local}(n)$ combined with the available source histories},
\begin{equation}
 \calD_i^{(n)}
 =\calD_i^{\rm local}(n)\cup\bigcup_{j\ne i}\calD_j^{\rm hist}.
 \label{eq:pooled_dataset}
\end{equation}
The GP and expected-improvement acquisition rule are otherwise unchanged from ordinary BO. This deliberately simple full-pooling baseline isolates the value of cross-system calibration information rather than introducing a new acquisition function or task kernel. It therefore tests whether transfer is already explained by objective alignment before introducing more expressive multi-task BO machinery. The search space is $\Xi=[-3,3]^3$, and every target begins with the same two-point Sobol initialization. \MGnew{Detailed GP settings are listed in Supplementary Section 2.}

\JWrev{We compare three methods.} \emph{Independent BO} uses only target observations. \emph{Global warm start} assigns all source records unit weight. \emph{Dynamics-weighted warm start} tests whether physically similar sources should be trusted more strongly. We additionally evaluate \emph{RGPE}~\citep{feurer2018rgpe} post hoc as an established transfer-BO comparator, using the frozen confirmatory source histories and target protocol. RGPE fits one GP per historical source task and one target GP and then weights their predictions according to posterior-sampled ranking loss on the observed target points. \JWrev{Local RLS identification yields the model vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i=\operatorname{vec}([\hat{\mathbf A}_i\;\hat{\mathbf B}_i]^\top)\in\mathbb R^6$ and precision $\hat{\mathbf W}_i$.} Without using family labels, we define
\begin{align}
 (\distdyn_{ij})^2
 &=\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_j\right)^\top
 \frac{\hat{\mathbf W}_i+\hat{\mathbf W}_j}{2}
 \left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_i-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_j\right), \nonumber\\
 s_{ij}&=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\distdyn_{ij})^2}{2\ell_d^2}\right),
 \qquad
 \alpha_{ij}=\frac{\alpha_0}{\max(s_{ij},0.02)},
 \label{eq:dyn_weighting}
\end{align}
where $\ell_d$ is the median pairwise-distance bandwidth and $\alpha_{ij}$ is the source-record GP noise. If dynamics similarity predicts transfer usefulness, this weighting should outperform global pooling.

\section{Experimental Design}

\paragraph{Vehicle benchmark and local identification.}
The benchmark uses a two-state lateral bicycle model for local LQI synthesis and a nonlinear tire-force plant for closed-loop evaluation, creating deliberate model mismatch. Each realization contains 30 systems from three families: \emph{nominal}, \emph{payload} with $m$ increased by 20\% and $I_z$ by 25\%, and \emph{tire-degraded} with $C_f$ reduced by 25\%. Within each family, $m$, $I_z$, $C_f$, $C_r$, and constant rollout speed $U$ receive independent $\pm2.5\%$ multiplicative scatter. \MGnew{The fleet generator admits only variants with a feasible baseline controller. No resampling was needed in the confirmatory fleets.} Family labels are never provided to BO. \MGrev{Each system identifies its discrete model by RLS over eight 6\,s episodes under a fixed nominal-model LQI controller, with forgetting factor $0.99$ and no added open-loop excitation.} \MGnew{Supplementary Sections 3 and 4 give the plant, identification, and feasibility details.}

\paragraph{Calibration task.}
\MGnew{Every BO query averages yaw-rate RMSE over six episodes: three stochastic repetitions for each lane-change half-duration $T_{\rm lc}\in\{1,2\}$\,s.} Candidates are infeasible if LQI synthesis fails, $|\beta|>6^\circ$, or \MGnew{the steering-angle increment exceeds $0.2618$\,rad per simulation step, equivalent to approximately $300^\circ$/s.} \MGnew{Feasibility is checked after each simulated rollout. Physical deployment would require an additional safety mechanism to prevent constraint violations during evaluation.} The same task distribution and constraints are used for source histories, target calibration, and the \JWrev{reference-controller search}.

\paragraph{Held-out evaluation.}
\JWrev{The evaluation uses 20 fleet realizations that were not used to develop the three primary methods.} In each realization, every system first contributes a 25-evaluation independent-BO history. Six systems, two per family, are then treated in turn as new targets. Their own histories are removed, leaving $29\times25=725$ source records. Each target receives two local Sobol evaluations and at most ten BO-selected evaluations, producing 120 target-calibration problems. The post-hoc RGPE comparison reuses these exact frozen histories, targets, and budgets without altering the primary methods. Fleet realization is the statistical replication unit. For the pre-specified comparisons, paired method differences are computed after averaging the six targets within each realization. The 95\% intervals use 10,000 bootstrap resamples, with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the same 20 realization-level differences. The same paired summaries are reported for RGPE only as exploratory post-hoc analyses, not confirmatory inference. A nested source-maturity study exposes $M_{\rm hist}\in\{2,5,10,25\}$ records per source while keeping the target protocol fixed.

\paragraph{Post-hoc calibration-landscape analysis.}
After observing that dynamics weighting did not improve the pre-specified comparison, we added an exploratory mechanistic diagnostic using the first five development-fleet realizations, selected by seed order rather than outcome. All 30 systems in each fleet are evaluated on one fixed 128-point Sobol design shared across systems and realizations. Each grid point is scored with the same six-episode calibration objective used by BO: three stochastic noise realizations for each of the two lane-change maneuvers. The maneuver distribution is common across systems, while the deterministic episode seeds are client-specific. For each pair, rank correlation is computed only on jointly feasible calibration points,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{ij}^{J}
 =\operatorname{Spearman}\!\left(
 \{J_i(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(m)})\}_{m\in\mathcal F_{ij}},
 \{J_j(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(m)})\}_{m\in\mathcal F_{ij}}
 \right),
 \label{eq:landscape_similarity}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_{ij}$ is the jointly feasible index set. \MGnew{The study contains $5\binom{30}{2}=2175$ within-realization system pairs.} These pairs are not treated as independent replicates: realization-level summaries are computed within each of the five fleets. Because only five fleets enter this post-hoc analysis, bootstrap intervals across fleets are used only as descriptive uncertainty summaries, not as confirmatory population inference.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Fleet warm start halves target-system experimentation}
\JWrev{Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} shows reference-relative regret over the target-evaluation budget.} All transfer methods improve over independent BO, with the two pooled warm starts converging fastest.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\figurepdf{0.88\textwidth}{figures/fig3_confirmatory_convergence.pdf}
\caption{Held-out convergence over 20 fleet realizations and 120 target-calibration problems. \JWrev{Curves show median reference-relative regret evaluated on separate test episodes.} Shaded bands show the interquartile range across target problems. RGPE transfers useful source information, while global and dynamics-weighted pooling converge fastest.}
\label{fig:convergence}
\end{figure}

Independent BO has a budget-restricted mean $N_{5\%}$ of 6.717 evaluations, compared with 3.083 for global warm start, a 54.1\% reduction. The paired realization-level difference is $-3.633$ evaluations with 95\% bootstrap CI $(-5.01,-2.37)$ and Wilcoxon $p=0.0005$. In the additional post-hoc comparison, RGPE has lower restricted mean target effort than independent BO, with $N_{5\%}=4.625$ and paired difference $-2.092$ evaluations. The corresponding exploratory paired summaries are 95\% bootstrap interval $(-3.52,-0.69)$ and Wilcoxon $p=0.0151$. Relative to global pooling, RGPE requires 1.542 more evaluations on average. The exploratory interval is $(0.92,2.28)$ with $p=0.0009$. Independent BO reaches the 5\% criterion within budget for 95 of 120 targets (79.2\%), RGPE for 118 of 120 (98.3\%), and both pooled warm starts for all 120. Detailed threshold-attainment curves are reported in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Moderate source maturity captures most of the benefit}
Figure~\ref{fig:maturity} varies the amount of historical experience available from each source. With $M_{\rm hist}=2$, the restricted mean $N_{5\%}=6.475$ remains close to independent BO. It decreases to 5.667 for $M_{\rm hist}=5$ and to 3.525 for $M_{\rm hist}=10$, where all 120 targets reach the criterion within budget. At $M_{\rm hist}=25$, the restricted mean keeps decreasing to 3.283.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\figurepdf{0.58\textwidth}{figures/fig7a_fleet_maturity_absolute.pdf}
\caption{Target-system sample efficiency versus source-fleet maturity. Historical source records are nested prefixes of independent-BO trajectories. Error bars use fleet realization as the resampling unit. The top axis reports the total number of source records available to a target.}
\label{fig:maturity}
\end{figure}

Ten evaluations per source recover approximately 93\% of the mean experiment saving obtained with 25 evaluations while using only 40\% of the per-source historical budget. The major gain therefore appears between five and ten evaluations per source. Deeper histories mainly improve the remaining tail of slower target calibrations. The $M_{\rm hist}=25$ sensitivity endpoint differs slightly from the main mature-fleet result because the nested study uses a common two-point source initialization rather than the main eight-point mature-history initialization.

\section{Discussion}
The experiments support a distinction between \emph{plant heterogeneity} and \emph{task-landscape heterogeneity}. The vehicle families are physically distinguishable, while the post-hoc landscape diagnostic shows strongly aligned LQI calibration rankings. In this regime, broad historical coverage is more valuable than either the tested dynamics-based attenuation or RGPE's target-adaptive source weighting. In the exploratory post-hoc comparison, RGPE has lower target effort than independent BO but remains 1.54 evaluations slower than global pooling. Its weights redistribute strongly and prune more sources as target data accumulate, consistent with spending scarce early target information distinguishing sources whose calibration landscapes are already highly aligned. More heterogeneous task distributions may instead favor selective or multi-task transfer.

The source-maturity result qualifies this conclusion. Two shared initialization points provide little benefit because they repeatedly sample the same locations across the fleet. Sequential source BO diversifies the historical coverage, and the major improvement appears between five and ten evaluations per source. Thus prior fleet experience is useful only after it has explored enough of the shared calibration space. Beyond that point, deeper histories primarily reduce tail risk.

The transferable information object is the task-level record $(\boldsymbol{\xi},\tilde y)$ rather than a plant model or feedback gain. At $M_{\rm hist}=10$, a target receives 290 such four-scalar records from 29 sources. This is compact relative to raw time-series data, but the reported 54\% reduction concerns \emph{incremental target-system experimentation}. The historical evaluations were generated during earlier commissioning and are not free.

Still, several limitations remain. Validation is simulation based, and the study considers one three-dimensional LQI parameterization for a lateral-control task within a typical, narrow constant-speed operating regime. Higher-dimensional controller classes or more heterogeneous task distributions may exhibit weaker landscape alignment and may favor selective or multi-task BO models. Feasibility is assessed from completed simulated rollouts rather than guaranteed before execution. Physical deployment would therefore require an external safety mechanism such as a certified safety filter, backup controller, or safe acquisition strategy. The dynamics-aware ablation uses one fixed distance-to-noise mapping (median distance bandwidth and a 0.02 weighting floor). We do not claim that its null result covers all dynamics-informed transfer schemes. \JWrev{The reference-controller search is numerically well converged but does not prove global optimality.} Finally, the work characterizes transferability and sample efficiency rather than privacy or communication-optimal distributed BO.

\section{Conclusion}
Fleet-informed BO reuses normalized controller-calibration observations across heterogeneous dynamical systems while retaining target-specific model identification and gain synthesis. Across 120 held-out vehicle calibration problems, this reduces the budget-restricted mean target effort from 6.72 to 3.08 evaluations. Ten historical evaluations per source recover about 93\% of the mature-fleet saving. In a post-hoc comparison, simple pooling also outperforms the RGPE transfer-BO baseline. \MGnew{Despite heterogeneous plant dynamics and personalized feedback gains, calibration landscapes are strongly aligned: 2174 of 2175 system pairs satisfy $\rho_{ij}^{J}\ge0.95$. This is consistent with feedback compensating for plant differences and helps explain why broad sharing of calibration observations is effective in the studied regime.}
